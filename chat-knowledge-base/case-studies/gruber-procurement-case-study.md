# Case Study — Gruber Procurement Intelligence Platform

## The opportunity

Purchasing operations across multiple companies can become fragmented among supplier files,
item and SKU lists, inventory records, spreadsheets, purchase orders, approvals, savings
projects, scorecards, and executive reports.

Fragmentation creates several operational problems:

- Teams may work from different versions of supplier and item data.
- Savings opportunities and follow-up actions are difficult to track.
- Supplier risk, performance, and data quality may not be visible in one place.
- Approval and decision history may be difficult to audit.
- Leadership reporting may depend on manual consolidation.

## Dave's approach

Dave studied the purchasing-transformation opportunity and mapped the information, roles,
decisions, and handoffs involved. He then designed a connected procurement operating
environment organized around a shared source of truth.

## Demonstration scope

- Supplier master
- Item and SKU master
- Purchase orders
- Inventory snapshots
- Savings pipeline
- Supplier scorecards
- Approvals
- Audit history
- Executive reporting
- Human-supervised AI assistants

## Design principle

AI is treated as an evidence-based assistant rather than an unsupervised decision maker.
Agents can help identify risk, data-quality issues, performance changes, and next actions,
while users remain responsible for review and approval.

## Result and context

The project demonstrates how disconnected procurement information could become a more visible,
accountable, and decision-ready operation. It also demonstrates Dave's process: identify
fragmentation, map operating relationships, organize the data model, and build a practical
system around the decisions people need to make.

This is a self-directed portfolio and process-design demonstration created for the Gruber
opportunity. It is not represented as a deployed internal Gruber production system.
